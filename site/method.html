How it works

What Relisted does

The US Consumer Product Safety Commission publishes a notice for every recall, with photos of the product. When a seller is told to stop, the same product often keeps appearing on other stores, other sites and other countries' storefronts.

Relisted takes the seller's photo from the notice, asks Google Lens which pages carry it, and sorts the answer into evidence you can open: store listings, the country each store serves, and the stores that sell in India. India has no single public recall database, and product safety sits with separate regulators, so a recall abroad does not automatically reach a seller there. It also searches the product's name on Google Shopping India, which shows whether a shopper there would find it by name.

From a recall to its listings

  • Recalls. The CPSC recalls feed is public JSON and needs no key. Cost: no SerpApi search.
  • Priority. Recalls of products sold by marketplace sellers come first, then children's products, then fire and battery hazards.
  • Photo. We pick the notice photo with the whitest border, because seller photos sit on a white backdrop. Photos taken on a lab table are skipped: no store uses them, so Lens can only return other products that look similar.
  • Search by photo. One Google Lens search through SerpApi, type exact_matches, on that photo. Cost: one SerpApi search.
  • Search by name. One Google Shopping search through SerpApi, country India (gl=in), for the product name in the notice. It counts the results whose title carries the recalled brand. Cost: one SerpApi search. Everything after the two searches is free and runs on the saved answers.
  • Sorting. Rules read each address and title and label every match, as the table below shows.
  • Trail. Per recall: listings by country, the stores that sell in India, what was left out, and a link to the raw search result.

Every search is saved to disk the first time it is paid for and never paid for twice. Without a SerpApi key the program replays the saved searches, so anyone can run the full demo offline.

How matches are sorted

LabelHow a match gets itCounts as a listing
Store listingA product page on a known store, judged by the shape of its address, such as amazon.in/dp/…Yes
ShopAn unknown site with a price or a product path, whose title mentions the recalled productYes
Store category pageA known store, but not a single productNo
NewsA news site, a news or blog path, or a title about the recall, deaths or injuriesNo
Social postFacebook, Instagram, YouTube, Reddit and similarNo
Copy on an unrelated siteAddress patterns used by hijacked sites that republish scraped listings, or a title in another script on an unrelated domainNo
OtherEverything elseNo

A listing counts as sold in India only when it is a product page on a known store that sells there: Amazon India, Flipkart, Desertcart India, Ubuy India, DHgate India and a few others. An unknown shop with a .in address does not count.

What it cannot tell you

  • A match is a lead, not a verdict. Lens matches the look of the photo. For a product with a distinctive shape, such as the teething toy on the home page, the matches are the same toy. For a plain-looking product, such as a pool drain cover or a coin battery, Lens also matches other brands' products. Compare the listing photo with the recall photo before you act.
  • A listing may be gone. The results are a snapshot. A page can be out of stock, or taken down after the snapshot.
  • Lens returns at most 400 matches for one photo and offers no further pages. Where a recall shows 400+, the true number is higher.
  • The country comes from the store's web address. Ubuy and Desertcart run a separate storefront for each country, which is why country counts run high. A plain .com address says nothing about where a shop sells, so those listings are grouped as country not shown.
  • The recalled name check uses the first word of the product name in the CPSC notice. A brand written as two words is matched on its first word only. Words that are ordinary English, such as Magnetic or Little, are not counted.
  • The name search reads one page. Google Shopping returns about 40 results per search and no further pages, so a seller using the name further down is not seen.
  • CPSC's long hazard paragraph is not used. Some records carry another recall's text: the teething-toy recall holds the hair-serum paragraph of the recall filed just before it. The reason shown on each page comes from the recall's title, and the page links to the notice.
  • The rules can be wrong. A small shop can be filed as a copy, and a copy can pass as a shop.
  • Recalls with lab photos only are not searched, so the sweep undercounts.

Nothing here says a seller knew about a recall or did anything wrong. The listings are for a person to review.

Reporting in India

  • The National Consumer Helpline takes complaints about defective products bought online: call 1915, or file on the portal or the NCH app.
  • Under the Toys (Quality Control) Order, 2020, toys for children up to 14 must meet Indian Standards and carry the ISI mark. The BIS Care app takes complaints about the mark.

Each recall page with listings on stores that sell in India repeats these, next to the listings, with a file of every listing to download.

Sources

  • Recall notices: the US Consumer Product Safety Commission recalls feed, public data. Each page links to its notice.
  • Image search: Google Lens, through the SerpApi Google Lens API. The raw response for each recall is published next to its page.
  • Name search: Google Shopping, through the SerpApi Google Shopping API, with India as the country. Its raw response is published too.
  • Listings and thumbnails belong to their stores. They are linked, and shown small for identification.
  • Typeface: Archivo, under the SIL Open Font License.

Run it yourself

The code is at github.com/tanayvasishtha/Relisted. With uv installed:

  • uv sync
  • uv run relisted serve, then open http://127.0.0.1:8000

With no key it replays the saved searches. To search live, put SERPAPI_API_KEY=… in a file named .env.

These are leads for review, not findings. A listing that matches a recall photo may be a different item, or may already be gone.
